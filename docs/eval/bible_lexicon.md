# The Bible's vocabulary, from the Latin text

Generated by `scripts/build_bible_lexicon.py`. Do not edit by hand.

The Clementine Vulgate is read word by word; each word is traced to its dictionary entry in the English Wiktionary's Latin (an inflected form through the entry that names its headword), and the entries are counted. Latin supplies the list and the count, never a form: the last two columns show which daughters have a reflex of the word and the shortest one. A reflex is whatever Wiktionary lists under the Latin entry, inherited or borrowed, and may have drifted in meaning; the modern Bibles are the check on that.

- Words in the text: 612,221. Traced to a dictionary entry: 568,209 (93%).
- Distinct dictionary words: 8,134. With a reflex in at least one of the 36 lects: 5,209; in ten or more: 1,400.
- The 100 commonest words are 55% of the text, the 500 commonest 75%, the 1,000 commonest 82%, the 2,000 commonest 88%.
- By part of speech: noun 3,519, verb 2,727, adj 1,286, adv 398, num 68, pron 37, conj 34, prep 28.
- A form that belongs to two words (*est* of *sum* and of *edo*) is counted for the commoner word.
